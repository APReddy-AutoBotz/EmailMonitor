## Staged deletion and purge

Stage one atomically writes a durable UUID/reason tombstone, fences current/future work, invalidates all tenant export leases/downloads and deletes private artifact bytes. A job marker increments its generation, clears running execution units, releases outstanding resolution reservations and removes request content. An organization-content marker immediately refuses every current/future job and source release; stage two processes at most10 jobs per call. Repeated stage/purge requests return the existing receipt.

Stage two removes observation/discovery content, author/candidate/evidence/association/review rows, content-bearing export snapshots/idempotency requests, and synthetic probe results. Inherited continuations referencing removed evidence are also fenced before references are cleared. Selected-URL lists and job-bound source request paths/session digests are removed. Job/root UUIDs, settled resolution accounting and its execution-item references, tombstones, membership/mock identity and bounded audit metadata remain. Execution items necessarily retain only the closed authored fixture URL constants plus state/counters for accounting and inherited references; they cannot retain arbitrary source URLs or article/contact content. Source-only admission records without a job retain fixed fixture paths and synthetic session digests until their existing120-second expiry/cleanup, and mock sessions follow their existing expiry/revocation lifecycle; this is not deletion of an IdP identity or a production account. Preserved root accounting cannot grant permission to revive content or recharge an already settled article. Private transaction contexts are removed within the same transaction and are unreadable/unforgeable by runtime.

Source claim, completion and final entitlement acquire organization SHARE NOWAIT before privacy checks and nested admission mutex use; a competing privacy UPDATE refuses rather than allowing stale release or reversing lock order. Current source completion and parse/export commit predicates must refuse a tombstoned scope. A job-filtered result read refuses that unavailable job; an otherwise authorized aggregate list omits its content. Stale export completion returnsfalse under the existing replay contract and cannot retain artifact bytes. Required controlled acceptance deletes the synthetic organization-content scope only after an actual pinned HTTPS response receipt, then proves no byte release or subsequent DNS. Required real SQL tests cover parse/lease/export interruption, direct-RLS concealment, repeated purge and empty downloads. A pure expected fixture is never that evidence.

## Retention and scheduling

Tenant ceilings follow document21: raw24h, excerpts30d, contacts90d, exports24h, audit365d and idempotency24h. Current/source reductions are intersections, never new rights. Zero contact/excerpt/export windows refuse the affected content immediately. Per-job retained deadlines only tighten, so increasing a later setting cannot unexpire old content; new exports capture their own expiry ceiling. The contact/excerpt windows conservatively start at job creation; retained excerpt expiry fences the whole affected job. Existing source/object expirations still apply. No raw archive is implemented: `raw_hours` is a future archive ceiling, not a claim that memory-bound acquisition creates a retained raw object. No indefinite contact retention or legal-hold override exists.

`tick` processes at most10 fenced/expired jobs and10 invalid private export snapshots, removes expired operational records, and records the next hourly due time. Immediate read/release refusal is independent of sweep cadence. `Lifecycle.schedule` atomically records due events; an identifier-only producer uses the existing owned fixture-I/O queue, confirms broker acceptance and may republish uncompleted events after broker loss. The worker rechecks current owner authority, applies a bounded sweep and completes the event atomically. Duplicate deliveries return the original count and cannot run another batch. The test assembly exposes reusable scheduling/worker entry points; no production scheduler or persistent worker is deployed.

Audit stores only actor/resource/event UUIDs and fixed action/reason codes. SQLAlchemy hides parameters; exact lifecycle denials are sanitized without preserving raw exception chaining. Do not log suppression inputs, excerpts, keys/digests, cookies or arbitrary exception payloads. Existing synthetic logs and snapshots are test artifacts, not customer records.

## Support and restore

Only the current owner approves/revokes a context, for an existing active same-tenant member and job, with fixed reason and maximum240 minutes. Creation and inspection lock relevant identity rows as well as memberships and recheck active state; lock contention refuses. Every inspection intersects current session, role/membership, owner approval, source/purpose, job/tombstone, expiry and revocation. Inspection returns bounded state/count/UUID metadata and appends a minimal audit event; it cannot return paper/contact content or bypass normal roles. No external support login or persistent grant is configured.

Backup tombstones must be replayed before any restored tenant becomes readable or workers/downloads resume. Restore source records cannot replace the live tombstone ledger, reset generation fences, unhide purged IDs or roll back settled accounting. The synthetic regressions preserve tombstones across service replacement/replay and refuse stale parsed commits/new work; actual backup infrastructure, expiry certificates, restore ordering and disaster-recovery execution remain EM-017. Tombstones and keyed suppression metadata may outlive content only for this restore/privacy purpose; a production retention/backup horizon is not invented here.
