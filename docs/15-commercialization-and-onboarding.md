# 15 — Multi-customer commercialization and onboarding

**Baseline:** 2026-10-08. This is a commercial operating hypothesis and product packaging plan. Prices, demand, source coverage and margins have not been validated. No subscription, contract or paid infrastructure has been created.

## Product proposition

A repeatable academic contact-research workflow: keyword/source or URL in; correct author/contact details, evidence, explicit gaps and export out. Sell tested source coverage, staff-time reduction, identity quality and governable operations—not 'unlimited emails from any website'.

Initial prospects: legitimate publishers, multi-journal operators and publishing-services teams with repeated search/extraction work. Qualify actual workflows, source rights, volume, field needs, deployment/security requirements and willingness to pay. A publisher portfolio or service team is the account, not every individual journal.

## Packaging model

| Package hypothesis | Customer need | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Validation pilot | Prove a narrow source/workflow and ROI | Paid scoped study, agreed sample/fields/gates, no implied production SLA |
| Hosted organization | Repeatable jobs, review, exports, team access | One tenant, configured quotas/sources, standard updates |
| Hosted portfolio/services | Several teams/projects, higher quotas and integrations | One or explicitly separated organizations, enterprise controls |
| Dedicated/private deployment | Customer-controlled network/data region/operations | Same software images, separate infrastructure and negotiated support |
| Connector/integration service | A new licensed website/API or downstream system | Separate discovery/implementation/certification scope |

White-label display and logos can be an entitlement; do not imply ownership of the code or unlimited sublicensing. Reseller/multi-client use requires contract and separate tenant boundaries. No raw cross-customer contact database is bundled.

## Pricing research and economics

Use implementation/onboarding fee + annual platform license or hosted subscription + included usage + transparent approved overage/support. Private deployment and custom connector support cost more to operate and should be scoped separately. Do not auto-select a payment gateway or implement card charging in P0; an entitlement/usage interface is sufficient.

Test willingness to pay through actual pilots. An illustrative quote worksheet should record: users/organizations, source families, article-resolution units, browser/PDF mix, retention/storage, deployment mode, support hours, source licenses, security reviews and expected manual-review burden. Any proposed price is a hypothesis until a buyer accepts it; do not repeat earlier conversation vendor price/market-size numbers as verified facts.

Unit economics: contribution = contract revenue minus allocated infrastructure, third-party source/data costs, support, connector maintenance, security/operations and acquisition costs. Track cost per usable contact and per attempted article separately. Not finding an email still consumes resources; disclose whether the chosen contract charges attempted resolution units or successful-contact units. Provider permission-denied requests blocked before acquisition must not be silently billed as a successfully resolved contact.

Usage ledger is independent of invoice generation. Default proposal: one article-resolution unit settled once per unique job/article after eligible work reaches a terminal outcome; retries do not create extra resolution units. Browser seconds, pages and bytes are operational costs with separate caps; charging them requires an explicit contract. No universal 'unlimited' plan before load/source-cost evidence.

## Customer onboarding checklist

1. Qualify source websites, keywords, sample URLs, desired fields and author-selection rules; obtain authorized test data and baseline manual effort.
2. Decide hosted/dedicated region and identity integration; agree tenant boundaries for parent company, subsidiaries and clients.
3. Establish legal entity, agreement, permitted purpose, processing roles, source/API rights, privacy handling, retention, export and support contacts.
4. Provision owner, roles, entitlements, source policies/credential references and branding. Verify least privilege and safe defaults.
5. Run a small permitted sample, review evidence/mapping, tune supported source configuration without changing the benchmark rules.
6. Train operators on no-contact versus blocked, review states, partial counts, export restrictions and source-support requests.
7. Complete UAT, security/restore checks and acceptance report; only then activate production workload and agreed billing.

Source policies approved once for a defined scope are reused until expiry/revocation; customers should not have to approve every article. Permission changes and new domains/routes require review.

## Support and customization boundaries

Supported: configuration, certified connectors, approved integrations, reusable feature flags and versioned extensions. Not supported: custom forks for every customer, circumventing restricted sources, guaranteeing source uptime, maintaining unbounded historical contact freshness, importing unlicensed lists, or using customer data to train shared models without a separate agreement.

Define source layout-change maintenance, standard versus custom connector responsibilities, supported software versions, patch cadence and response commitments. A solo operator should not promise round-the-clock support without a staffing plan. Keep customer-specific contracts and budgets out of this public repository.

## Commercial validation gates

At least three qualified design-partner conversations and at least one paid/contractually committed pilot are recommended before significant commercial expansion. Measure accepted price, integration effort, source-license cost, contact quality and review minutes. These are proposed go/no-go gates, not claims that customers exist.

## Expansion roadmap

After a reliable extraction product: approved wider-web enrichment, saved/recurring searches, reviewer/guest-editor discovery use cases, then governed outreach and inbound workflow integrations. Reuse the tenant/source/evidence foundation rather than letting later features delay the core product.
